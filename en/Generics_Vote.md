### Vote for <> syntax

  * [Bram](</index.php?title=User:Bram&action=edit&redlink=1> "User:Bram \(page does not exist\)") 14:14, 9 Nov 2005 (CET)
  * [Gadnio](</index.php?title=User:Gadnio&action=edit&redlink=1> "User:Gadnio \(page does not exist\)") 18:15 28 Nov 2005 (GMT+2)
  * [Wanderlan](</User:Wanderlan> "User:Wanderlan") 15:26, 7 November 2006 (CET). See another example already implemented in Object Pascal at [Chrome Syntax](<http://www.chromesville.com/articles/?id={A1D08EE3-0D9E-4828-AFB3-B2C1E772186E}>). Simple, Clean and Elegant. With out unnecessary reserved words as **Generic** and **Specialize**. Perhaps Delphi follow this syntax. An another option use '()' instead '<>' for more Pascal compliance (No C-ism), similar to [Schema Types](<http://www.gnu-pascal.de/gpc/Schema-Types.html#Schema-Types>) (Generics Pascalism) in Extended Pascal (ISO-10206) and GNU Pascal.  
  
FPC's generics are actually more than the simply generics implemented by .Net languages. FPC's implementation is more like C++ templates which are much more powerful though they has been limited to avoid the ugliest C++ styled template hacking --[FPK](</User:FPK> "User:FPK") 20:25, 7 November 2006 (CET)
